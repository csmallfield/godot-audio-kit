# patch_convert

Direct conversions of synth patch formats into `AKPatch`, checked against reference renders. See `docs/plans/synth-expansion.md`.

## DX7

1. **Reference renders.** `python tools/patch_convert/render_msfa.py [library]` renders each DX7 voice in a prep-tool library (`tools/open_samples_prep`) with msfa's FM engine (Dexed's "Modern" engine, Apache-2.0) as `render_msfa_v100.wav`. It uses the layout in the sidecar's `content.render`, and needs `D:\tools\dx7render\dx7render.exe`, which the prep tool builds.
2. **Convert and check.** `godot --headless --path . --script res://tools/patch_convert/dx7_batch.gd -- [library]` writes `gak_patch.tres` and `gak_report.json` beside each sidecar, and `gak_dx7_summary.json` in the library. The report holds the SNR per note against the msfa render, plus caveats.

`dx7.gd` maps each DX7 operator to oscillator N:
- the frequency through `dx7_freq`;
- the algorithm through `fm` lists from `AKPatchLib.dx7_algorithm`, and `feedback` = 4π / 2^(8 − feedback);
- each operator's amplitude block onto its own `dx7` envelope (env2–env7, through `level_env`);
- the pitch envelope onto env8 (`dx7_pitch`), and the LFO onto `lfo1` (`mode = "dx7"`);
- the gain to 1/8, as `dx7render` scales its output.

**Caveats.** `dx7render` zeroes each note's engine state (LFO phase, sample-and-hold state, feedback buffer), so the references are reproducible. The one caveat left is feedback 7, which can be chaotic: float and msfa's integers can drift apart, and noise voices (snares, Train, Wasp Sting) can't be matched sample by sample. Weak voices without a caveat are quiet ones, where the 16-bit reference is the limit (an error floor near −95 dBFS).

## Synth1

1. **Catalogue.** The prep tool (`tools/open_samples_prep`) writes a sidecar per `.sy1` with every parameter named (`content.synth1`).
2. **Reference renders.** `D:/tools/dawdreamer/venv/Scripts/python.exe -I tools/patch_convert/render_synth1.py [library] [--jobs n] [--force]` renders each patch with Synth1 itself, through DawDreamer, as `render_synth1_v100.wav` (16-bit stereo) and adds `content.render_synth1` to the sidecar. Default library: `D:/12_library/gak/processed_synth1`. The layout is the DX7 one: notes 36, 48, 60, 72 and 84 at velocity 100, each held 2 s, released for 2.5 s, then 0.25 s, at 48 kHz and 120 bpm. Unchanged patches are skipped.

**How it renders, and why.**
- Synth1 under DawDreamer crashes (an access violation) as soon as two voices sound at once: a plain chord on a fresh instance, or a note starting while the last one still releases. So each note renders alone, on a fresh instance, for its own slot (a tail longer than the slot is cut there), and the slots are joined. A fresh instance per note also means nothing carries over between notes or patches.
- Each patch renders in its own process, several at once (`--jobs`), so a crash costs only that patch. A poly arpeggiator whose steps overlap still crashes; such a patch is rendered again in mono mode and flagged (`forced_mono`, and a note): its steps cut each other's release.
- Old patches: Synth1 changed what some values mean over its versions (the filter envelope amount became bipolar, the delay's level became dry/wet, the chorus rate and feedback and the unison detune were rescaled), and converts an old patch's values when it loads it. The chunk carries a version (byte 720), so the renderer writes the file's `ver=` there and Synth1 converts exactly as it does on loading the file. A file without `ver=` predates 1.05 and is loaded as version 100 (its gain knob sits at 64 where versioned files sit at 107; version 100 turns 64 into 93). The values Synth1 then holds are recorded in `content.render_synth1.values`, and the converter reads those.
- Synth1 can go well past full scale. A render louder than that is scaled down to fit and the file records `scale`: divide by it for Synth1's real level.
- Synth1 isn't repeatable sample by sample (free-running oscillators unless "osc phase" is set; noise, unison and S&H are random), so the check compares loudness envelopes and spectra, not samples.
- About 4.6 MB per patch: 3.3 GB for the 713-patch subset, about 76 GB for all 16,706.
3. **Convert.** `godot --headless --path . --script res://tools/patch_convert/synth1_batch.gd -- [library]` converts each rendered patch (`synth1.gd`) and renders it the same way, each note alone for its slot: `gak_patch.tres`, `gak_render_v100.wav` and `gak_convert.json` (converter version, caveats, the file's scale) beside each sidecar.
4. **Check.** `D:/tools/dawdreamer/venv/Scripts/python.exe -I tools/patch_convert/check_synth1.py [library]` compares the two renders per note (its hold and release): `level_db` (GAK against Synth1, the median over 10 ms frames), `env_err_db` (the loudness envelope's mean error once that level is out) and `spec_err_db` (the spectral shape's mean error across third-octave bands, per frame, each frame's level out). Both are floored 60 dB under Synth1's peak. It writes `gak_report.json` per patch and `gak_synth1_summary.json` in the library.

5. **The whole library, in chunks.** `D:/tools/dawdreamer/venv/Scripts/python.exe -I tools/patch_convert/synth1_chunks.py [library] [--size 1000] [--jobs 16] [--chunks n] [--keep-renders]` runs steps 2–4 over the patches not yet done, a chunk at a time (render the references, convert and render with GAK split across `--jobs` Godot processes, check), and then deletes the chunk's two WAVs. What stays beside each sidecar: `instrument.json` (with `content.render_synth1`: the values Synth1 held and the layout), `gak_patch.tres`, `gak_convert.json` and `gak_report.json`. A patch with a `gak_report.json` is done, so a stopped run picks up where it left off; the summary counts every patch done so far, from its report. Progress goes to `gak_synth1_chunks.log` in the library, and each chunk's list to `.chunks/`: `render_synth1.py --list`, `synth1_batch.gd` with the list and `check_synth1.py --list` redo a chunk (for a better converter). On 24 cores a chunk of 1,000 takes about 20 minutes, mostly Synth1 (each note on a fresh instance).

`synth1.gd` maps every Synth1 parameter through the curves measured in `tools/synth1_ref/data` (see `docs/plans/synth-expansion-phase0-synth1.md`, "Measured curves"):
- osc1 (its 8-voice detune as an 8-copy stack), osc2 (pitch from the measured table, fine, fixed when untracked, sync, ring), FM into osc1, the sub as a third oscillator taking its share of osc1's level, unison;
- the filter types (LPDL as the ladder), cutoff, resonance as the q giving Synth1's peak, key tracking, the filter envelope on env2;
- the amp envelope (a linear attack, exponential decay and release as time constants), gain, velocity, both LFOs (free or synced) and the mod envelope (env3) as matrix rows;
- play mode, portamento, bend range, polyphony, pan, the arpeggiator;
- the effect (saturate, crush, ring, compressor or phaser), the EQ, the delay and the chorus.

Synth1's FM is a fixed deviation in Hz, so its index falls as the note rises; GAK's index is fixed, so it is matched at middle C. Pulse waves play at 0.43 of GAK's level (Synth1's pulse sits 2.5 dB under its saw, GAK's 4.8 dB over). `caveats(params)` lists what a patch only approximates.

## Helm

1. **Catalogue.** The prep tool writes a sidecar per `.helm` file with its settings and modulation rows (`content.helm`).
2. **Reference renders.** `D:/tools/dawdreamer/venv/Scripts/python.exe -I tools/patch_convert/render_vsynth.py helm [library]` renders each patch with Helm 0.9.0 itself (VST2, through DawDreamer) as `render_helm_v100.wav`, in the Synth1 layout (notes 36–84 at velocity 100, 2 s held, 2.5 s release, 48 kHz, 120 bpm), each note on a fresh instance. Default library: `D:/12_library/gak/processed_helm`.
3. **Convert.** `godot --headless --path . --script res://tools/patch_convert/vsynth_batch.gd -- helm [library | list.txt] [part/parts]` converts each patch (`helm.gd`) and renders it the same way: `gak_patch.tres`, `gak_render_v100.wav`, `gak_convert.json`. Run several parts at once (`0/12` .. `11/12`).
4. **Check.** `D:/tools/dawdreamer/venv/Scripts/python.exe -I tools/patch_convert/check_vsynth.py helm [library]`: Synth1's metrics per note, `gak_report.json` per patch and `gak_helm_summary.json` in the library.

`helm.gd` works from Helm's own formulas (its source: stored value, then the parameter's skew; modulation amounts added before the skew). Files older than 0.9 are upgraded first, as Helm's loader does.
- **Oscillators.** osc 1 and 2 as wavetables of their Fourier series (GAK's polyBLEP saw and square fall 2–3 dB under Helm's tables at 10–13 kHz), the step and pyramid waves from one naive cycle; unison as the oscillator's `stack` (up to 8 of Helm's 15 voices, at Helm's spread and level), harmonize as a table of the harmonic series the voices land on. Cross modulation is two silent sines at the oscillators' pitches phase-modulating each other and the oscillators (π × amount radians). The sub is a table of its whole period (shuffle squeezes one of its two cycles; frames across shuffle when modulated). Noise; the oscillator feedback as `comb`.
- **Filter.** The 12 and 24 dB blends as the state-variable `mix` (Helm's equal-power LP → BP → HP weights; 24 dB with the square root of q per stage), the shelf style as `lowshelf`, `bandshelf`, `highshelf` (resonance is their gain); cutoff as a MIDI note, key tracking about MIDI 64, the filter envelope (env2) in semitones. The drive is the gain tanh(drive × x) has on the oscillator mix, simulated over 4,096 samples. Helm smooths the cutoff from 80 after a patch loads, so a note on a fresh instance still hears the end of that glide: env4 on the cutoff.
- **Envelopes and LFOs.** Times are the stored value squared; decay and release reach 1/256 in that time (a time constant of t / ln 256). lfo1, lfo2 the mono LFOs (free-running ones at their place 0.1 s in, where the references' notes start), the poly LFO and the step sequencer (smoothing worked into its points) as custom LFOs in `lfos`.
- **Modulation.** Rows to cutoff, resonance, pitches, volumes, cross mod, LFO rates and sub shuffle become matrix rows in GAK's units. Volumes are squared in Helm: an envelope on an oscillator's or the noise's volume becomes a stages envelope (power curves at −ln 256, Helm's exponential) that the oscillator follows (`level_env`) and that also sets its level, which is exact; LFO rows, and several rows on one volume, are linearised. A row on the filter envelope's sustain or depth, or an LFO on its own amplitude, is worked out to first order; an LFO on the filter blend gives the blend's mean weights. Anything else acts through its source's mean over 2 s of a held note.
- **Playing.** Polyphony, mono and legato, portamento (always mode), bend range, velocity tracking (negative too, through a velocity → amp row). The arpeggiator maps (as played becomes up); Helm's arpeggiator keeps time at 44.1 kHz whatever the host's rate, so at 48 kHz its steps run 48/44.1 fast, and the conversion matches that.
- **Effects.** Distortion as `saturate` (soft clip exact: tanh at the drive, output at tanh(drive)), delay and reverb (both Freeverb-style in Helm too: feedback → room, damping → damp, the √ dry/wet law), and the formant filter as four peaking EQs with a high- and low-pass, fitted to its response over the vowel square (about 3 dB per band).

**Caveats** (`caveats(content)`): stutter isn't converted; GAK's reverb damping stops at 0.4 of Helm's range (128 of the 216 factory patches sit above it, mostly at Helm's default 0.5); rows to the comb, the unison detune, effect settings, envelope times and LFO waveforms or tempos aren't modulated; a dry/wet above 0.5 (delay) or 0.8 (reverb) leaves the dry too loud; the arpeggiator retriggers envelopes from their level, where Helm restarts them from zero. Unison phases, noise and sample-and-hold are random in both, so those patches can't match sample by sample.

On the 216 factory patches (converter version 2): level −0.01 dB (median); envelope error median 2.02 dB, p90 3.69; spectral error median 3.51 dB, p90 5.61. The 32 patches without caveats: envelope 1.31, spectral 2.01 dB.

## OB-Xd and OB-Xf

1. **Catalogue.** The prep tool (`tools/open_samples_prep`) writes a sidecar per OB-Xf patch and per OB-Xd bank program, with OB-Xf 1.0.3's 102 parameters normalized 0..1 by name (`content.obxf.params`). OB-Xd programs go through `osp/obxd.py`, a port of OB-Xf's own importer.
2. **Reference renders.** `D:/tools/dawdreamer/venv/Scripts/python.exe -I tools/patch_convert/render_vsynth.py obxf [library]` renders each patch with OB-Xf 1.0.3 (VST3) itself, its parameters set by index, as `render_obxf_v100.wav`, in the Synth1 layout (each note on a fresh instance). Default library: `D:/12_library/gak/processed_obx`.
3. **Convert.** `godot --headless --path . --script res://tools/patch_convert/vsynth_batch.gd -- obxf [library | list.txt] [part/parts]` converts each patch (`obxf.gd`) and renders it the same way. Eight processes with `0/8` .. `7/8` do the 1,620 patches in about 3 minutes.
4. **Check.** `D:/tools/dawdreamer/venv/Scripts/python.exe -I tools/patch_convert/check_vsynth.py obxf [library] [--list file]`: the Synth1 metrics, `gak_report.json` per patch and `gak_obxf_summary.json` in the library.

`obxf.gd` follows OB-Xf's engine formulas (`src/engine/SynthEngine.h` and the voice code), checked against OB-Xf renders of single settings through DawDreamer (oscillator, noise and pan levels, filter responses against resonance, envelope curves, LFO phases and synced rates):
- **Oscillators.** Each oscillator's saw, pulse (or the triangle when both are off) is a GAK oscillator of its own, at 1.5 × the mixer level (OB-Xf's waves are 1 V peak to peak and its mixer multiplies by 3). The pulse is inverted with `width` = OB-Xf's duty, so saw and pulse line up as OB-Xf sums them. Osc2 takes pitch, detune, the PW offset, cross mod (`fm_mode = "pitch"` from osc1's parts, 24 × the knob semitones per unit, plus the constant shift a pulse's DC offset makes), sync to osc1 and env → pitch and PW. Ring mod is a copy of osc2 ring-modulated by each of osc1's parts, with the pulses' DC terms added to the other oscillator's level. Noise at its measured level; brightness as `pre_filter.lowpass_hz`, matched at the −3 dB point, with a 12 Hz high-pass for OB-Xf's DC blocker.
- **Filter.** The 2-pole filter is the state-variable `mix` (LP → notch → HP, or LP → BP → HP with BP blend), q = 1 / (2 (1 − r)) from OB-Xf's resonance curve, push as 0.035 less damping. The 4-pole filter is the ladder: resonance 0.875 r (OB-Xf's k = 3.5 r), `poles` = 4 − 3 × mode, the Xpander modes as `pole_mix` straight from OB-Xf's table, `drive` 0.1 (OB-Xf's ladder is all but linear) and its make-up gain. Cutoff, keytrack (pivot F3) and envelope amount (140 semitones × the envelope) as OB-Xf computes them.
- **Envelopes.** Both are stages envelopes: the attack a power curve through OB-Xf's RC/linear blend, the decay a power curve with amount ln(level ratio), which is exactly OB-Xf's exponential decay toward 0 stopped at the sustain, the release falling to −100 dB in its time (its curve set for the key-up level after the references' 2 s), all normalized to OB-Xf's 0.9 peak and delayed 16 samples as OB-Xf's are. Velocity to the filter envelope is `level_velocity`, to the amp `velocity_sens`.
- **LFOs.** Each LFO's sine/triangle and square/saw blend is drawn as a `custom` LFO (scaled into −1..1, the rows scaled back), its S&H or S&G as a `random` or `smooth` LFO of its own; rates, tempo sync (OB-Xf's rate values, not its labels), LFO 1 free-running from its measured phase, LFO 2 restarting per note. Mod amount 1 to pitch and cutoff, amount 2 to PW and volume (a dip, never a boost: its constant part goes into `gain_db`).
- **Voices.** Polyphony, unison (detune spread evenly over the expected range of OB-Xf's random offsets, voice pans as `spread`, 10 log10 N dB), mono or legato from the envelope legato mode when one key takes all voices, glide (OB-Xf's one-pole time constant), bend range. `voice_pan` from the pan knobs (balance matched to OB-Xf's linear pan) and `slop` from the slop knobs, divided by √N when a GAK voice holds N unison voices.
- **Two make-ups.** OB-Xf's oscillators are brighter at the top than GAK's (its minBLEP, and its aliasing): an `eq` high shelf, +3.75 dB at 12 kHz (+1.5 in HQ mode). And OB-Xf's oscillators run free from random phases where GAK's start together, so two oscillators near one pitch start 6 dB up where OB-Xf's average 3: osc2 starts a quarter cycle on (`phase` = 0.25). The parts of one OB-Xf oscillator (saw, pulse, osc2's ring copies) share its start phases through `phase_with`, under unison too. `nyquist_clamp` is on, as OB-Xf holds each oscillator under 0.45 cycles a sample.

**Caveats** (`caveats(content)`): S&H and S&G LFOs are random (266 patches); note priority low/high (143, mono patches); ring mod of a modulated pulse takes its DC terms at the knob's width (128); OB-Xf's fresh voice glides its first note from A6, so references of slow-glide patches start with a sweep GAK can't make (70); bend on osc2 only (63); the 2-pole filter near self-oscillation, where OB-Xf's diode damping (and push past it) isn't modelled (58); unison above 8 voices (39); pink and red noise played as white (49); pulse width past 99% (26); split envelope legato modes (23); both LFOs to volume (16). Not per patch: vibrato (mod wheel squared × an LFO, which the matrix can't multiply) and glide slop aren't converted.

**Results** (converter version 2). On all 1,620 patches: level 0.07 dB (median); envelope error median 2.19 dB, p90 4.22; spectral error median 3.07 dB, p90 5.06. The 1,046 without caveats: envelope 1.99, spectral 2.66 dB. The 132 unison patches with a saw + pulse oscillator, now aligned: envelope p90 3.94 dB (4.66 before), spectral 4.00 (4.08), p90 5.75 (6.12). OB-Xf isn't repeatable from one instance to the next (oscillator phases, unison detune, the slop offsets and S&H are random per voice), so a second OB-Xf render of the same patch, compared with the reference, gives envelope 2.10 and spectral 2.85 dB (median of 240 patches): the converted patches sit 0.11 and 0.20 dB above that floor.

## TAL-NoiseMaker

1. **Catalogue.** The prep tool writes a sidecar per TAL-NoiseMaker program (`content.tal`: its attributes, the drawn envelope's spline points as `curve`, and the program's own XML).
2. **Reference renders.** `D:/tools/dawdreamer/venv/Scripts/python.exe -I tools/patch_convert/render_vsynth.py tal [library]` renders each program with TAL-NoiseMaker 5.0.6 (VST2) itself, the program loaded as the plugin's state, as `render_tal_v100.wav`, in the DX7 and Synth1 layout. Default library: `D:/12_library/gak/processed_tal`.
3. **Convert.** `godot --headless --path . --script res://tools/patch_convert/vsynth_batch.gd -- tal [library | list.txt] [part/parts]` converts each program (`tal.gd`) and renders it the same way: `gak_patch.tres`, `gak_render_v100.wav`, `gak_convert.json`.
4. **Check.** `D:/tools/dawdreamer/venv/Scripts/python.exe -I tools/patch_convert/check_vsynth.py tal [library]`: the Synth1 metrics, `gak_report.json` per patch and `gak_tal_summary.json` in the library.

`tal.gd` follows the open port's formulas (DISTRHO-Ports `tal-noisemaker`), checked against 5.0.6 by rendering single settings through DawDreamer. What 5.0.6 does differently, measured:
- It reads five attributes by new names: `velocityfilter`, `lfo1Phase`, `lfo2Phase`, `envelopeOneShot`, `delayfactorR`. A program saved with the port's names (`velocitycutoff`, `lfo1phase` ...), as the whole factory bank is, plays those at 0, so the converter reads the attributes from `program_xml` by their exact names.
- The cutoff is the square of TAL's sum (knob, key follow, envelopes, LFOs), clamped after squaring: a negative sum opens the filter again.
- The drawn envelope runs 48000 / 44100 times faster at 48 kHz (16 / speed beats a cycle nominally). Synced delay times are the port's (twice the note for straight notes).

The mapping:
- **Oscillators.** osc1, osc2 and the sub (a square two octaves down, the sync master for both) with TAL's levels ((20^v − 1) / 19), tunes, pulse width and polarity (its mixer subtracts osc1 and osc2 from the sub). Their start phases (osc1 half a cycle on, by default) are the oscillators' `phase` (not under sync, where the sub's first wrap restarts them; setting them there matched slightly worse). `nyquist_clamp` holds every oscillator under Nyquist, as TAL clamps at 22 kHz. Ring modulation is a copy of osc2 ringed by osc1, crossfaded as TAL does. FM is osc2's frequency moved by a sine at osc1's pitch: an index of 10 × amount in radians, plus the carrier's shift (10 × amount × f1) as semitones.
- **Filter.** LP24/18/12/6 are the ladder with 4–1 `poles`; HP, BP and notch are its `pole_mix`es; the SV types the state-variable filter at 24 dB. Cutoff, resonance and each type's level are measured against 5.0.6. Drive is the ladder's drive with TAL's gain staging. Key follow is a straight line in octaves through TAL's curve. A resonance at 0.9 or more gets a quiet noise to start the self-oscillation that TAL's internal noise starts.
- **Envelopes.** All three are stages envelopes on TAL's own curves: an exponential approach to 1.04 + A/2 for the attack, exponentials toward just under 0 for the decay and release (power curves, exact). The filter envelope goes through TAL's cutoff curve in octaves. With the free AD on the filter too, their sum is sampled in time as one envelope.
- **LFOs.** Both LFOs with their waves, rates, tempo sync, phase and key trigger. LFOs to the cutoff become `custom` shapes through TAL's curve. Pitch, PW, FM, volume, pan and the other LFO's rate are matrix rows. The drawn envelope is sampled from its Bezier into a 256-point `custom` LFO (`once` for one-shot).
- **Playing.** Voices, mono or legato, portamento, velocity, bend range and detune (as `slop`).
- **Effects.** The master high-pass is a one-pole `filter` effect (always on: 7.6 Hz at 0). The delay's loop filters are matched at 500 Hz, the first echo too. The chorus is Juno-style (0.7–4.9 ms; I, II or both at 1.4 × wet). The reverb's decay is matched to 5.0.6's measured RT60. The bitcrusher (it truncates osc1 + osc2 before the filter) is converted as the level it leaves only; a crush effect after the voices matched worse.

**Caveats** (`caveats(content)`): on the 259 patches, the chorus's wet low-pass (130 patches); key follow as a straight line (112); detune fixed per voice slot (101); cutoff modulators taken through the curve one at a time (87); reverb input filters (83) and predelay (77); the delay's loop filters (67); glide linear in TAL (48) and the first note's glide up from F#2 in "on" mode (12); modulated FM's carrier shift (48); vintage noise as white noise (44); bitcrusher level only (13).

On the 259 patches: level 0.0 dB (median); envelope error median 2.27 dB, p90 4.85; spectral error median 4.37 dB, p90 7.98. The 5 patches without caveats: envelope 0.25, spectral 1.04 dB.
